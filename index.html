PROBLEM & SCOPE

Joining part of a trip needs a complete agreement

The brief highlights a gap: group trips assume everyone travels together, while partial joiners coordinate in chat. Pickup, cost and confirmation become separate conversations. I focused on bringing those decisions into one joining flow.

Riya joins four friends on their Bengaluru–Madikeri trip at Mysuru or Kushalnagar. She needs to know where to meet and what she owes. Ananya, the organiser, needs to review the pickup and revised split before confirming her seat.

MVP scope · One friend, one onward journey
Choose an existing pickup stop, review travel and stay costs, agree, and request a seat. Include approval, cancellation and decline states, plus pickup details and map access. Return travel, flexible stay dates, booking and payments sit outside this flow.
ASSUMPTIONS

The rules that keep the split understandable

An existing trip, a known group
Ananya has already planned the route, timings and stay. Riya joins through an invitation; trip creation and booking happen elsewhere.
Two pickups, no detour
Mysuru and Kushalnagar are planned stops. Riya reaches either pickup independently and stays for both nights; this version covers partial travel, not a shorter stay.
Travel varies; stay is fixed
Riya shares fuel and tolls only from her pickup onward. The earlier leg costs her ₹0. A separate ₹1,500 contribution covers the two-night stay.
Agreement before confirmation
Riya agrees to the estimate before sending a request. Ananya checks seat availability and confirms; only then does the new split apply.

DESIGN DECISIONS

Four choices that make joining easier to judge

01
Trip card: variation 3 keeps the first choice simple
Variation 1 brought pickup controls onto the card. Variation 2 added a route summary alongside date details. I chose variation 3: clear “Leaving” and “Back” dates, the route and the group, with one invitation CTA. Pickup choices come after the friend has opened the trip.
02
The route is one tap away in a bottom sheet
An inline itinerary lengthened the page and repeated the pickup details. The bottom sheet keeps stops, times and estimates together on the main screen, while “View route” provides the full journey when needed. Closing it returns the friend to the same decision.
03
A request starts with explicit cost agreement
“Request to join” stays disabled until the friend ticks the agreement checkbox. Travel costs and the fixed stay contribution are visible first, so Ananya receives a request with an agreed estimate. Changing the pickup resets consent. The checkbox records agreement; no payment is taken.
04
Show “₹160 less each” beside the revised split
New totals alone make Ananya work out the benefit. For the Mysuru pickup, “₹160 less each compared to before” makes the saving for each existing traveller explicit. It explains what changes for the group, while the confirmation note makes clear that the new split is still conditional.
FINAL DESIGN

A clear pickup, an agreed share, a confirmed seat

Try both pickup options, review the split, and switch to Ananya’s view to confirm or decline. The confirmed plan keeps the meeting point, time and map access together.

TRADE-OFFS

The limits of a focused first version

Fixed stops limit pickup flexibility
Choice
Offer Mysuru and Kushalnagar, already on the route.
Cost
The friend must reach a listed stop, even if another location is more convenient.
Why
Protect the group’s route and timing while making two pickup options easy to compare.
Manual approval adds a wait
Choice
Confirm a seat only after the organiser reviews the request.
Cost
The friend cannot treat a submitted request as a reserved seat; a slow response can delay their plans.
Why
Capacity and group readiness need a human check. Separate pending and confirmed states keep that dependency visible.
Upfront agreement stops short of settlement
Choice
Agree to estimated travel costs and a fixed stay contribution without collecting payment.
Cost
Actual travel costs may change. Final reconciliation and renewed agreement for changes are not covered yet.
Why
Resolve the joining decision first, while keeping the estimate and fixed charge distinct.
SUCCESS METRICS

Measure confidence at each joining step

Three proposed targets cover completing a request, understanding the cost and knowing where the request stands.

≥80%

Unassisted request completion

Invited joiners choose a pickup, review the share and send a request without help.

≥90%

Correct cost understanding

Joiners explain their travel share, the excluded earlier leg and the fixed stay contribution.

≥90%

Request status clarity

Joiners correctly identify whether their seat is pending, confirmed or declined, and know what to do next.

WHAT’S NEXT

Validate the flow. Make the agreement reliable.

01
Validate both sides of the agreement
Test the finished prototype with five joiners and five organisers. Check pickup choice, cost understanding, pending versus confirmed status, and recovery from cancellation or decline. Fix observed confusion before adding features.
02
Define what happens when the plan changes
Work with product and engineering on late arrivals, full seats, unanswered requests and revised costs. Decide when requests expire and when a change needs fresh consent, then design those states.
03
Validate whether the split feels fair
Review both pickup options with real travel groups. Check whether joiners and organisers understand and accept the travel split and fixed stay contribution. Use disagreements to refine the cost rules and explanation.
04
Connect request updates to trip notifications
Notify the organiser when a request arrives and the joiner when it is approved or declined. Link each update to the current trip state, keeping pickup details and the agreed estimate consistent for both people.
